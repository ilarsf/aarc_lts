    <section id="river-turn" class="river-turn-section" aria-labelledby="river-turn-heading">
        <p class="rules-eyebrow">The maneuver to rehearse</p>
        <h2 id="river-turn-heading"><i class="fas fa-sync-alt"></i> River Turn</h2>
        <p>All boats should use a controlled river turn at assigned turn points, dams, or bridges. Your coach will name the exact cue for the session.</p>
        <ol class="river-turn-steps">
            <li><strong>Stay right.</strong> Hold the correct traffic side as you approach.</li>
            <li><strong>Stop.</strong> Come under control before starting the turn.</li>
            <li><strong>Turn left.</strong> Make the first 90-degree turn.</li>
            <li><strong>Check and cross.</strong> Cross only when traffic is clear.</li>
            <li><strong>Turn left again.</strong> Rejoin the correct side for the return.</li>
            <li><strong>Clear the area.</strong> Move out of the turn zone and wait for coach direction if needed.</li>
        </ol>
        <p><strong>Example near the dam:</strong> Row until the coach-assigned cue, stop, make your river turn, row across only when clear, turn again, and take strokes to clear the turning area for others. If multiple boats are turning, leave extra room for the boat closest to shore.</p>
    </section>

    <section class="hazard-grid" aria-labelledby="hazard-heading">
        <div class="hazard-grid-heading">
            <p class="rules-eyebrow">The hazards to expect</p>
            <h2 id="hazard-heading"><i class="fas fa-exclamation-triangle"></i> Scan before steering</h2>
        </div>

        <article>
            <h3><i class="fas fa-users"></i> Other river users</h3>
            <p>We share the river with Pioneer, Huron, and U-M men's crew teams, plus canoes, kayaks, paddle boards, fishermen, and coach launches. Be predictable, courteous, and ready to stop.</p>
        </article>

        <article>
            <h3><i class="fas fa-swimmer"></i> Swimmers</h3>
            <p>Swimmers can be hard to see, especially in summer. Watch carefully near the dock and along mixed-use stretches. Stop if the path is unclear.</p>
        </article>

        <article>
            <h3><i class="fas fa-leaf"></i> Weeds and shallows</h3>
            <p>Weeds become more prevalent as summer progresses, especially around bends and shoreline edges. Give them room while staying in the traffic pattern.</p>
        </article>

        <article>
            <h3><i class="fas fa-archway"></i> Bridges and current</h3>
            <p>Bridge openings, pillars, branches, and current require early decisions. Review the bridge reference only when your coach assigns that route.</p>
        </article>
    </section>
